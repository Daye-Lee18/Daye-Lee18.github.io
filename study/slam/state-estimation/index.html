State estimation lineage — from LiDAR to LIVO and leg-sensor fusion

Paper comparison anchored on FAST-LIO2: sensors, estimation architecture, loop closure, and the Vision60 application perspective.

처음 방문했다면: 이 페이지는 입문 목차가 아니라, 기본 개념을 실제 시스템에 연결하는 비교표다. SLAM, 좌표계, registration, filter/graph의 차이가 아직 낯설면 먼저 90분 빠른 입문 코스를 읽는다.

추천 읽기 순서

  1. FAST-LIO2에서 현재 기준 시스템의 입력, state, residual, local map을 찾는다.
  2. LIO-SAM과 비교해 filter와 graph가 어떤 상태를 보관하고 고치는지 본다.
  3. 실제 문제에 따라 Point-LIO(빠른 운동), FAST-LIVO2(카메라), VILENS(leg sensor), LTA-OM(loop/장기 mapping) 중 하나를 고른다.
  4. 각 리뷰를 읽을 때 “더 최신인가?”보다 어떤 failure mode를 어떤 관측으로 줄이는가?를 묻는다.

This is the first study page for understanding the principles and limits of FAST-LIO2, currently deployed on Vision60, and for judging what information to add on steps, slips and impacts. The core comparison set is FAST-LIO2, FAST-LIVO2, VILENS, LIO-SAM, LTA-OM, Point-LIO and GLIM, together with the prior work that explains the lineage and the recent follow-up research.

Sources checked: 2026-09-07. This is a curated list of work relevant to the project, not an exhaustive ranking of the latest papers. The structure and contributions of each paper are kept separate from the hypotheses about applying it to Vision60. Accuracy and runtime figures from papers with different sensor configurations, compute platforms and datasets are not compared as a single number.

1. Core paper comparison

L is LiDAR, I is IMU, V is camera, and K is joint/leg kinematic information. Loop closure means re-observing a past place to correct accumulated error. The inclusion column refers to the system covered by the paper itself; separate extension packages are noted as such. If the table is wider than the screen, it scrolls horizontally.

**FAST-LIO2** · T-RO 2022 L + I / tightly coupled iterated EKF ① Direct scan-to-map registration of raw points
② Efficient iterated filter update
③ Incremental insert, delete and rebalance in the ikd-Tree		 Builds a local point cloud map. The original paper has no global loop closure or pose graph correction The current baseline. Trace IMU propagation → deskew → point-to-plane residual → state and map update. Measure geometric degeneracy separately from accumulated drift

② Feature-based local scan matching

**Point-LIO** · Advanced Intelligent Systems 2023 L + I / point-by-point filter ① State update at each point’s measurement time
② Motion model that treats the IMU as an observation
③ Handles high-bandwidth, aggressive motion		 Focused on odometry and mapping. A global loop back-end is separate The comparison group for impacts and fast rotation. **FAST-LIVO2** · T-RO 2025 L + I + V / sequential ESIKF update ① Sequential fusion of LiDAR geometric and image photometric residuals
② A unified voxel map linking points to image patches
③ Plane priors and exposure estimation to strengthen image alignment		 LiDAR and image share the same map. The original paper focuses on odometry rather than global loop correction The candidate for adding a camera. Check the conditions under which images compensate for weak LiDAR geometry. Examine darkness, blur and texturelessness together with time synchronisation and extrinsics
**VILENS** · T-RO, online 2022 / issue 2023 V + I + L + K / factor graph ① Tightly coupled fusion of four sensor modalities
② Preintegration factor on leg velocity
③ Online linear velocity bias estimation		 Centred on multi-sensor odometry. Using a factor graph does not by itself mean place recognition or loop closure is included The key comparison for slip and leg information. Read the rough-terrain experiments on ANYmal and the meaning of the velocity bias. Check the accessibility of Vision60’s joint and contact information and its kinematic model
**LTA-OM** · JFR 2024 L + I / FAST-LIO2-based LIO + global correction ① STD-based loop detection and correction
② Rejection of false-positive loops
③ Long-term association reusing the corrected past map		 Includes loop closure, long-term mapping and multi-session localisation and mapping. The corrected past map is fed back into LIO registration The comparison group for the modularity goal. Read the boundaries between front-end, loop detection and global correction, and the path by which the corrected map is reused
**GLIM** · Robotics and Autonomous Systems 2024 L + I, supports multi-camera constraints / fixed-lag smoothing + global optimisation ① GPU-accelerated scan matching factors
② Joint optimisation of the states within a time window
③ Global minimisation of inter-submap registration error		 A mapping system that connects local odometry to global submap optimisation The comparison group for optimisation-based robustness. Check how it exploits past states through temporary geometric degeneracy, and the GPU and memory burden

The direct in FAST-LIO2 does not mean geometric residuals or correspondence search are skipped. It means raw points are registered to the map without a preceding edge/plane feature extraction step. The image-direct approach of FAST-LIVO2 uses photometric error instead of feature descriptor matching. Read the two carefully to distinguish what each takes as its observation residual. FAST-LIO2 paper, FAST-LIVO2 paper

The filter family concentrates on updating the current state and its uncertainty, while the smoothing family optimises states at several instants together. But filter versus graph and odometry versus loop-closing SLAM are different axes of comparison. A global back-end can be attached to FAST-LIO2, and there is odometry that uses a graph, as in VILENS. LTA-OM is a case of connecting a filter-based front-end to global correction. VILENS paper, LTA-OM paper

2. Prior work for understanding the lineage

Paper Place in the lineage What to compare against the next paper
LOAM: Lidar Odometry and Mapping in Real-time · RSS 2014 The LiDAR-centred starting point that separated high-rate odometry from low-rate precise mapping. IMU aiding optionally available Compare feature-based registration and the two-stage structure against LIO-SAM and FAST-LIO2
FAST-LIO · preprint 2020 Tightly couples LiDAR feature points and IMU in an iterated EKF. Efficient Kalman gain computation based on the state dimension Why FAST-LIO2 keeps the filter base while changing direct registration and the map data structure
FAST-LIVO · IROS 2022 Sparse-direct fusion of LiDAR, inertial and image data Which problems the unified map, sequential update and image alignment improvements of FAST-LIVO2 solve

Set the study flow as LiDAR registration → LIO tightly coupled with inertial data → LIVO adding images → state estimation using leg information. This does not mean every method is a direct successor of the previous one, or that later means better. Within LIO the design already splits between the graph of LIO-SAM and the filter of FAST-LIO, and VILENS is a separate branch combining four modalities. Loop closure and long-term mapping are examined independently of the number of sensors.

4. Reading the tables against Vision60’s problems

What follows are not conclusions about paper performance but application hypotheses to be confirmed by experiment. The first step is to pinpoint when and why FAST-LIO2 fails on the same log. Point cloud distortion, height drift and velocity jitter are different phenomena; do not assume one correction fixes them all.

Phenomenon to observe Papers to read in comparison Data to check first / criteria for judgement
Point cloud bends or pose jumps on landing impact and fast rotation FAST-LIO2 → Point-LIO Per-point timestamps, IMU saturation and dropouts, time synchronisation, deskew results. Measure output rate and end-to-end latency separately
Directional drift along walls, corridors and open ground FAST-LIO2 → FAST-LIVO2, GLIM Directions with weak geometric constraints, image texture and blur, relative error per segment. Compare the benefit of adding images against its computational cost
Velocity or height error grows on slips and steps VILENS → LIJO, with Legolas as support Synchronisation of joint angles, joint velocities, contact estimates and IMU; disagreement between kinematic velocity and external estimates. Evaluate slip and non-slip segments separately
Pose jitter at rest or low speed propagates into the control input FAST-LIO2 → LIJO Pose and velocity variance and frequency content over stationary segments, and the latency after filtering. Check that a smooth output is not erasing real motion
Maps fail to overlap on revisiting after a long traverse LIO-SAM → LTA-OM, GLIM Trajectory and map consistency before and after the loop, false detections, pose discontinuity on global correction. Separate revisit correction from momentary estimation failure

The front-end turns sensor input into a continuous pose and velocity plus a map for registration; loop detection proposes past place candidates and relative transforms; the back-end corrects the global trajectory and map with the accepted constraints. This is a functional split for the sake of comparison. In real implementations the corrected map returns to the front-end, as in LTA-OM, so you have to check not just the module names but which frame’s states, point clouds and constraints are passed at which moment. LTA-OM paper

For this study, reading in the order FAST-LIO2 → LIO-SAM → Point-LIO → FAST-LIVO2 → VILENS → LTA-OM → GLIM widens the design space outwards from the current system. Each paper review is organised under three headings: ① states and observation residuals, ② the failure mode addressed and the experimental evidence, ③ the inputs and interfaces to add on Vision60. Which recent extension to take up depends on whether the observed problem is closer to compute resources, slip or jitter.

5. Bibliographic details and interpretation notes

FAST-LIO2 was a 2021 preprint and a 2022 T-RO publication. FAST-LIVO2 was published online on 2024-11-19 and is cited as a 2025 paper in T-RO volume 41. For VILENS it is enough to distinguish the 2022 online publication from volume 39, issue 1 of 2023. FAST-LIO2, FAST-LIVO2 publication record, VILENS publication record

The GLIM paper itself is from 2024. The ICRA 2022 paper linked from the authors’ repository is prior work that GLIM builds on, and should be kept distinct. Also, rather than declaring FAST-LIVO2 the one current standard or summarising FAST-LIO2 as merely “the method that gave up the back-end”, compare the estimation scope each paper covers and the modules that can actually be added. GLIM authors’ repository

  1. FAST-LIO2 — paper review Registers raw LiDAR points directly against a local map and fuses them with IMU in an iterated filter; the baseline of the current Vision60 system.
  2. LIO-SAM — paper review Builds LiDAR-inertial estimation as a factor graph so GPS and loop constraints can be handled together; the smoothing-based control group.
  3. Point-LIO — paper review A high-bandwidth LIO that updates the state at each point's measurement time instead of waiting for a scan to complete.
  4. FAST-LIVO2 — paper review A LIVO that links LiDAR geometry and image intensity in the same voxel map and fuses them sequentially in an ESIKF.
  5. VILENS — paper review A quadruped state estimator that fuses visual, inertial, LiDAR and leg odometry in a graph and estimates a leg velocity bias.
  6. LTA-OM — paper review A long-term mapping system that connects loop detection, rejection and correction plus reuse of the past map to FAST-LIO2.
  7. GLIM — paper review A mapping framework that optimises states within a time window and inter-submap registration, handling the computation on GPU.
  8. LOAM — paper review The starting point of the lineage: separating high-rate odometry from low-rate precise mapping made real-time LiDAR estimation possible.
  9. FAST-LIO — paper review Combines LiDAR feature points and IMU in an iterated EKF, handling many observations efficiently; the basis of FAST-LIO2.
  10. FAST-LIVO — paper review Combines LiDAR, inertial and image data in a sparse-direct manner; the prior work behind FAST-LIVO2.
  11. Lightweight FAST-LIVO2 — paper review Reduces the memory and computation cost of FAST-LIVO2 by controlling observation usefulness and how much of the map is retained.
  12. Legolas — paper review Learns odometry from leg and inertial sensors alone, addressing situations where external-sensor tracking is difficult.
  13. LIJO — paper review Combines LiDAR, IMU and joint velocity information in an EKF to reduce high-frequency jitter in quadruped odometry.
